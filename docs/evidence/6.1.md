# Card 6.1: capture calls, `scripts/tune_current.py`

Branch `card-5`, based on 93b945a. `scope.c`, `sim.c` and the protocol are unchanged.

## What was built

- `gui/tune_api.py`: `capture_channels`, `capture_period_us`, `capture_trigger` (constants `RISING`,
  `FALLING`), `capture_start`, `capture_arm`, `capture_wait`, `capture_max`, `capture_final`,
  `capture_rise_us`, `capture_length`, `capture_value`, `capture_save`, all in `__all__`. Built on
  `Scope.start(..., mode 1, auto=False)`. The session has a mode (`stream` or `capture`, set by the first
  `channels()` or `capture_channels()`); mixing them is an error at the script line.
- `capture_start()` runs the checks of `stream_start()` (allow, safe_value, names, `tune_alive`, the verified
  target of the probe, limit variables measured), with the capture checks instead of the UART load:
  channels (at most 4), a trigger on a captured variable, and the transfer time at the baud rate (at most 1 s).
  Then the trial: one complete capture without a trigger, no script write before it, heartbeat on,
  timeout = window + transfer + 500 ms, scaled by `time_scale`. It must have exactly `depth` samples starting
  at 0, else the run is refused. `limit()` is checked on it too.
- `capture_arm()`: `Scope.start` with the trigger, then waits `pretrigger samples x period + output lag`, scaled
  (`Scope` cannot tell when the pretrigger buffer is full). `capture_wait(timeout_ms)`: 0, or -1 after
  `timeout_ms x time_scale` of wall clock; a timeout leaves the session usable (`return` ends the run with the
  safe end). `wait_ms()` is the scaled wall clock in capture mode. `write()` works during a capture, with no cursor.
- `limit()` is checked on the whole buffer after every `capture_wait()` that returns 0 (and on the trial). An
  excess aborts the run; the capture is kept as an unfinished trace.
- Abort in `capture_wait()` or during the transfer: safe values twice, `Scope.stop`, read-back, the heartbeat
  thread ends. A capture that arrived and was not saved is kept as `unfinished`.
- `capture_save()` writes `trace_NNN.csv` like `trace_end()` (physical columns from `unit()`). Time zero is the
  trigger sample. The CSV has `time_us` below a period of 1 ms, else `time_ms`. The event and `summary.png`
  take ms (the axis label of `render_png` is ms). `trace_failed()` before `capture_save()` marks the capture.
  A capture whose header does not match the request (samples, pretrigger) is invalid, so is its trace and the
  next row. `quality()` has a third member for this.
- `capture_max`, `capture_final` are raw integers; `capture_final` is the mean of the last 10 %, truncated
  towards 0 like C. `capture_rise_us`: 10 % to 90 % of the step from the mean before the trigger to
  `capture_final`, searched from the trigger sample, to the sample (no interpolation), -1 if there is no step
  or a level is not reached.
- The heartbeat measures the gap between its writes; `run.txt` gets one line at the end: largest gap in all and
  around a capture, in wall clock and target time, and the 1000 ms the target allows.
- `gui/tune_check.py`: recorders for all capture calls with a state (none, armed, have). New codes:
  E219 (transfer longer than 1 s at the baud rate; this is the UART fit of a capture), E227 (period),
  E228 (trigger missing or wrong), E314 (`read()` in capture mode), E315 (stream and capture calls mixed),
  E316 (calls out of order). E218 (f32 trigger) and W203 (`limit()` in capture mode) are raised now; the
  `RESERVED` tuple is gone. The dry run answers 0 for `capture_wait()` and every value.
- `scripts/tune_current.py` is the appendix of the plan, byte for byte (compared with the appendix text).

### capture_wait(): the timeout is about data that does not come

Before: the timeout counted the whole wait in scaled wall clock, so a transfer that was under way but slow was cut off
(the 6.1 runs 1 and 2 on MDB: the dump arrived 13 s after the step, the timeout was 12.5 s).

Now (`Session._cap_wait`): until the first byte of the dump the timeout counts as before, from the call, in scaled
wall clock. Once the dump is under way, the time counts only while no new byte has come: every new byte starts it
again. A transfer in progress is never timed out, one that stops still is, and the abort flag still ends the wait at once.

What the Scope offers: a new counter `Scope.dump_bytes` (bytes of the capture dump under way, header and data, 0 before
the header, reset by `start()` and by a sync byte that is not a header). It is counted in `Scope._read_exact`.
`_read_exact` used `ser.read(rest)`, and `MdbSerial.read(n)` holds everything back until n bytes are there or its
timeout of 15 s is over, so the counter stood at 4 for the whole 12.5 s on MDB (instrumented run: first byte at
+2.6 s, `n=4` until the timeout). `_read_exact` now reads what has arrived (`max(1, min(rest, in_waiting))`), as
`_stream` does. The result is the same on a COM port.

Tests (`tests/test_script.py`, port `SlowDumpSerial`: the dump of the armed capture in slices of 400 bytes, one per
300 ms, `read(n)` waits for n bytes as `MdbSerial` does; `capture_wait(300)` is 1200 ms of wall clock at scale 4):

### Flaky `tests/test_script.py`

There was one cause for the abort and for the gaps, and it was in the test port, not in the product. `SimSerial._advance`
took `cbuf.raw[:n]`, and `.raw` copies the whole 1 MB buffer with the interpreter held: 0.37 ms each time (median of
2000, maximum 1.19 ms). At 115200 baud a byte comes every 87 us, so the reader of 970513f (`_read_exact`:
`read(max(1, min(rest, in_waiting)))`) always found a byte and never reached the sleep in `read()`. It only let go of the
interpreter for the few us of the DLL call, and the other threads did not get it back until the dump was over.

Measured inside a script on SIM, scale 1, in ms. The sum of the four, to the end of the dump, is about 450 in every run:

| | wait_ms(100) | write(0) | printf | capture_wait |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before, 23 runs | 108 to 448 | 1 to 185 | 0 to 193 | 0 to 327 |
| after, 15 runs | 107 to 113 | 0 to 1 | 0 | 342 to 360 |

Ruled out: the garbage collector (0 collections in 5 runs, `gc.disable()` changes nothing) and sleep stalls of the machine
(`time.sleep(0.001)` alone in a process: largest gap 3.2 ms in 6 s). A sampler thread at 1 ms showed gaps of 33 to 298 ms
before and none above 30 ms after.

Fix: `ctypes.string_at(self.cbuf, n)` in `tests/sim_serial.py`. On a COM port and on MDB `SimSerial` is not used. The same
loop on a COM port has not been measured; `in_waiting` there is a system call and `read()` blocks, so the reasoning does
not carry over either way.

The three reported failures:

- `abort came during the transfer (captures arrived: [1])` and `abort during the transfer: ok`: test. The test slept 200 ms
  after the step and expected the dump to be still under way. With the script thread late by up to 340 ms that was a race.
  Now `HeldDumpSerial` gives 1200 of 4101 bytes and holds the rest until the safe end stops it. The test waits until 1000
  bytes have arrived and aborts then, so the abort is in the transfer in every run (`[0, 1198]` in all runs).
- `3 writes, largest gap 995 ms`: this check did not fail on the gap. Its bound was 1200 ms; it failed on `len(beats) >= 4`,
  because 3 captures at scale 4 last about 2.5 s and an 800 ms interval gives 3 or 4 writes. So a test fault, twice over: the
  count raced the length of the run, and the gap came from the starved thread (800 + up to 208 ms).
- What the code guarantees: the next write is due 200 ms x scale after the last one returned, then one poll step and the
  time until the thread runs. There is no hard limit, it is a wall clock loop in a thread. Measured now: 811 to 816 ms.
  At the nominal scale 4 the 1000 ms of `tune_block.h` are 4000 ms of wall clock, so 1008 ms is 252 ms of target time and safe.
  But `sim.dll` runs in real time, the scale is only what the test port says, so in this test the timeout is 1000 ms of wall
  clock and the old gaps were 8 ms from or past it. The bound is now that 1000 ms, for each gap. The script waits 4 s more, so with
  every gap below 1000 ms it has at least 6 writes (9 in all runs).

Also found: `BadHeaderSerial` needed the 4 header bytes in one `read()`. The fixed reader takes them in pieces, so it now
changes the byte as it passes (3 FAIL in one run before this, which is not counted below).
