# M7e-2: why tune_speed.py differs between SIM and MDB

Base 3e1026e (card-m7e merged). Changed: `scripts/tune_speed.py` section 3, two constants. Scripts and outputs in
`docs/evidence/m7e2/`. Everything ran on the simulator or the PC model. Nothing here says anything about hardware.

## Result

The cause is in the script. `BACK_MS = 50` is shorter than the time the speed needs to come back to the base speed
after a step (45 to 52 ms for the slow pairs). The next step therefore starts from a speed that is still moving, and
how far it has come depends on how much target time the writes in between cost: 0 to 10 ms on SIM, 22 to 102 ms on
MDB. The two ports measured the same model from different starting points. On top of that, `POLL_MS = 2` reads every
second sample, and the overshoot peak of the stiff pairs lasts about one sample, so whether it is seen depends on the
phase of the step against the poll grid.

Fix: `POLL_MS = 1` (every sample) and `BACK_MS = 200` (the speed is back before the next step). After the fix, on one
run of each, all 20 rows are within one poll step: settle_ms differs by at most 8 ms, overshoot by at most 1 point
(2 rows). The pair in `tuning.h` is the same before and after the fix, and on both ports: `SPEED_KP 500`,
`SPEED_KI 150`.

## 1. The old runs, trace by trace

Run folders of M7e section 2, copied to `docs/evidence/m7e2/data/old_sim` and `old_mdb` (results, run.txt, tuning.h,
traces). `python docs/evidence/m7e2/compare_speed.py docs/evidence/m7e2/data/old_sim docs/evidence/m7e2/data/old_mdb`,
output in `old_compare.txt`. The step is the sample where `motor.iq` jumps (the proportional part of the step).
"gap" is the target time from the write before the step to the step write (run.txt), "v0" the speed one sample
before the step, "pre" and "post" the largest speed difference of the two traces aligned at the step, 20 samples
before and 100 after.

The other 13 rows: pre and post at most 9 counts. Where the two steps start from the same speed, the traces agree to
1 to 4 counts after the step. Where they do not (rows 1, 4, 6, 12), the difference before the step carries through.
After the back step the speed had not come within 6 counts of 1500 for good by the end of the trace (45 to 52 ms
later) for every kp 200 pair, for kp 500 / ki 300, and on SIM for kp 500 / ki 60 (computed from the old traces).

Three mechanisms, each with the rows it explains:

- Start state (rows 1, 4, 6, 12): SIM steps from a speed up to 103 counts off the base speed, MDB from within 6. Row 1
  on SIM starts 103 counts low, so the trace does not settle in 100 ms (censored at 100), on MDB it settles at 58.
- Settling band edge (rows 2, 16): with band 6 counts, the SIM speed sits at -6 to -8 from 70 ms after the step on in row 2,
  the MDB speed at -2 to -6. A difference of 2 counts moves settle_ms from 52 to 100 (censored). Row 16, 40 to 100 ms
  after the step: 4 to 8 counts on SIM against 3 to 7 on MDB, 60 against 44 ms. The difference of a few counts is the left-over start state.
- Poll phase (row 20 and the 1 point overshoot rows): the traces agree within 2 counts, the peak is at sample 6 after
  the step on both. The script applies its rule to every second sample. Re-measured from the step sample (column
  "from step" of `old_compare.txt`): 38 on both; from the script's own start: 32 on SIM, 38 on MDB. The SIM step landed
  3 samples after the start of the measuring, so the polls fell on the odd samples and missed the peak.

The 2 to 6 ms differences of the other rows: the step lands 1 to 6 samples after the measuring start on SIM and -1
to 3 on MDB (column "delay" of `old_compare.txt`), so the SIM settle_ms is a few ms longer.

## 2. What was excluded

- The model code. `docs/evidence/m7e2/replay.py` replays the writes of a run (run.txt, step writes at the sample
  where the trace shows them) into `firmware/tune_model.h` compiled alone with gcc (`model_dll.c`, stepped in whole
  ms) and compares the speed with the traces (`replay.txt`). With a write delay of 4 ms the largest difference per trace
  is 1 to 3 counts in all 20 traces, on both ports. MDB runs the
  firmware built by XC-DSC at optimisation level 0 (16 bit int), SIM the same header in sim.dll by gcc -O2 (32 bit
  int); both are reproduced by the same PC build to the same accuracy, so they compute the same model. The header
  casts every product to int32_t and clamps before every narrowing cast; no int16 overflow was found by reading it.
  The 1 ms model tick and the sample clock are independent on both ports; their phase moves a write by at most one ms,
  which is the residual of the replay.
- Script timing as such. The cursor rule works as decided: the measuring starts at the sample after the write
  returned, on both ports. The writes cost much more target time on MDB (mailbox chunk), and that was harmless for
  the step itself; it only changed how long the speed had to recover before the next step.
- The measuring window. Rows that hit 100 on one side (1, 2) were censored because of the start state and the band
  edge, not because the window is too short. The rows at 100 on both sides (pairs that do not settle in 100 ms) are
  equal on both. A wider window was not needed; `HOLD_MS` stays 100.
- The ELF. MDB ran `test.X/dist/sim/production/test.X.production.elf` of 2026-11-01 21:09, the same as in M7e. It is
  older than fb0d836 (PLL dividers in `example_main.c`) and 22757ef (comments in scope.c), newer than the last change
  of `tune_model.h` (08e546a). The model in the ELF is the current one. Not rebuilt, to keep the M7e comparison on the
  same binary.

## 3. The fix, checked on the PC model first

`docs/evidence/m7e2/emulate.py` is the measuring logic of tune_speed.py on the bare model, with a chosen target time
per write (0, 5, 40, 75, 150 ms) and a chosen landing of the step (0 to 6 ms after the measuring start). Largest
difference to the case "0 ms, landing 0" over all 35 combinations, 20 rows each:

| POLL_MS / BACK_MS | settle_ms | overshoot |
|---|---|---|
| 2 / 50 (old) | 46 ms | 7 points |
| 2 / 200 | 6 ms | 5 points |
| 1 / 150 | 9 ms | 1 point |
| 1 / 200 (new) | 7 ms | 1 point |

`emulate.txt` holds the old and new tables at the five write costs. `START_SETTLE_MS` 400 instead of 200 made no
improvement (58 against 21 differing overshoot cells of 700), so it stays. Change in `scripts/tune_speed.py`:
line 116 `POLL_MS = 1`, line 118 `BACK_MS = 200`, with a comment on why.

## 4. The new runs

Each once, `tools/run_script.py --port SIM scripts/tune_speed.py` and `--port MDB --elf
test.X/dist/sim/production/test.X.production.elf scripts/tune_speed.py`. Copies in `data/new_sim`, `data/new_mdb`,
comparison in `new_compare.txt`. Status ok on both, no E401, no stream stop, no retry. Wall clock: SIM 16.3 s
(was 10.5), MDB 413.4 s (was 353, 437.9 s with start-up). Largest heartbeat gap: SIM 218 ms, MDB 252 ms of target time.

(overshoot_pct / settle_ms.) Against the rule: settle_ms within 10 ms in 20 of 20 rows (largest 8). Overshoot equal
in 18 rows, 1 point apart in 2. The aligned traces: speed before every step 1495 to 1504 on both, after the step
within 3 counts in 19 rows (row 4: 15 counts at the last sample, where the back step lands one sample earlier on SIM).
`tuning.h`: `SPEED_KP 500`, `SPEED_KI 150` on both.

Left over:

- settle_ms on SIM is 1 to 8 ms longer in 12 rows, never shorter. The step lands 2 to 7 samples after the measuring
  start on SIM and -1 to 2 on MDB (column "delay"). Likely cause: on SIM the newest received sample, which starts the
  measuring, is older than the model by the samples still queued for the UART; `SimSerial.output_lag_ms` is 0. Not
  verified, and not fixed: the lag varies by several ms, a constant would not make it deterministic.
- overshoot 1 point apart in 2 rows: one point is 3 counts on a 300 count step and the script rounds down, so a peak
  near a boundary flips with the 1 to 2 counts the aligned traces still differ by (the write lands within a ms).
  The decision of 3.4 allows differences in the time column only; whether 1 point of overshoot counts as a finding is
  for the supervisor.
